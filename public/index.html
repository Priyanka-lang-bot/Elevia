A wrong answer is a symptom, not a diagnosis

Students often follow a rule that looks right to them. Marking it wrong, or re-teaching the whole chapter, doesn't find that rule.

  1. 1

    Marks say what, not why

    "2/5" is wrong, but a score of 2 out of 4 doesn't show that the student added the tops and the bottoms separately.

  2. 2

    One mistake can be a slip

    A single wrong answer might be a typo or a rushed moment. The same rule showing up again is what makes it worth checking, and even then ELEVIA asks new questions before it calls it a gap.

  3. 3

    The real gap is often earlier

    Trouble adding fractions can come from an earlier skill, like finding common denominators. That's where the lesson should start.

How ELEVIA works

Four roles take turns with each student, then a retest checks the result. Every decision follows fixed, testable rules, so the same answers always lead to the same outcome.

  1. 1

    Suggests a possible gap

    Diagnoser

    Reads the five check-in answers and matches each wrong one against 86 named misconception patterns. A single mistake is flagged as a possible slip.

    2

    ½ + ⅓ = 2/5 matches "adds the tops and the bottoms".

    Its output is always suspected. Nothing is taught yet.

  2. 3

    Verifies before teaching

    Checker

    Asks two brand-new questions aimed at the same misconception.

    4
    • Confirmed the same mistake again
    • Rejected both right, so it was probably a slip
    • Inconclusive mixed, so a teacher decides

    The Checker also runs the retest later. It's the only part that can mark a skill mastered.

  3. 5

    Chooses the focus

    Planner

    Works only on a confirmed gap, and refuses one that hasn't been verified. It walks the skill map's prerequisites to decide where the lesson should start.

    6

    Adding with unlike denominators needs common denominators, which needs equivalent fractions.

    A teacher can change the focus. The change is logged with their reason.

  4. 7

    Teaches the missing step

    Tutor

    A short lesson that quotes the student's own wrong answer, then steps, worked examples and practice questions with hints and full solutions.

    8

    "Earlier you answered 1/2 + 1/3 with 2/5. That matches a common mistake… Let's see why it doesn't work and fix it step by step."

    Maths in examples is computed exactly, never typed by hand.

  5. 9

    Checks it stuck

    Retest

    After a delay (24 hours by default), three questions the student hasn't seen. Two out of three closes the gap and marks the skill mastered.

    10

    Fewer than 2 of 3? The gap stays open and ELEVIA plans a fresh lesson.

    The check-in questions are never reused.

What's built

Nine working parts. Each one runs in the current version and is covered by automated tests.

  • Misconception detection

    Wrong answers are matched to named misconceptions, each tied to the skill it points to.

    86 patterns across 26 topics

  • Verify before you teach

    No lesson is planned for a gap until new questions, or a teacher, confirm it.

    2 new questions per suspected gap

  • Plans from a skill map

    Prerequisites decide where a learning plan starts, including links across topics, such as percentages back to decimals.

    58 skills with prerequisites

  • Step-by-step lessons

    Worked examples, sense checks, and practice with hints and full solutions.

    55 lessons

  • Delayed retests

    Retests come after a gap of time (24 hours by default) and use new questions, never a repeat of the check-in.

    3 unseen questions, pass with 2

  • Progress you can trust

    Only a passed retest marks a skill mastered. Progress is saved after every answer, so closing the tab loses nothing.

    Resumes at the same question

  • Teacher review

    Teachers see a queue of unclear cases with the reason each one needs them. They confirm or reject a gap, or change the focus, and every decision is logged with a reason.

    Own classes only

  • Consent and privacy

    Parent consent for under-18s, school class codes with teacher approval, and adult learners choose exactly what to share. Every view of a student's progress is logged.

    Parents see only what consent allows

  • Exact answer checking

    Exact fraction arithmetic, accepted-answer lists, and code answers verified by running each snippet when the question bank is built. A student's typed code is compared as text and never run.

    1,019 questions validated

Verify before you teach

ELEVIA's central rule: a suspected misconception is only a hypothesis until new evidence supports it.

The usual way

  1. Wrong answer
  2. Assume the cause
  3. Re-teach the chapter
  4. Hope it worked

Fast, but it can teach the wrong thing.

The ELEVIA way

  1. A wrong answer matches a known pattern
  2. A suspected gap is noted. Nothing changes yet
  3. Two new questions test it
  4. The gap is confirmed, rejected or inconclusive. Inconclusive goes to a teacher
  5. A lesson is planned only for a confirmed gap
  6. A later retest, on new questions, checks whether it stuck

About ELEVIA

ELEVIA tries to find the missing understanding behind wrong answers, rather than only marking them right or wrong.

It's a working prototype built for PRASUNEX Innovathon 2026. It hasn't been tested in classrooms yet, so we make no claims about learning outcomes.
